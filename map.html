    processor

    China

    Number 1
    net importer of rare-earth compounds: 76,746 t imported vs 41,615 t exported, net −35,131 t (china_net_trade_by_stage.csv, 2024, tonnes)
    Number 2
    net exporter of magnets: 141,823 t exported vs 2,733 t imported, net +139,090 t (china_net_trade_by_stage.csv, 2024, tonnes)

    China now buys rare-earth compounds abroad, mostly from Myanmar, refines them at home and sells magnets to the world: the monopoly moved up the chain instead of ending.

    Source: Pietro, china_net_trade_by_stage.csv; Anna's texts, "The two monopolies"; Framework, Part B, B6.

    buyer

    Japan

    Number 1
    direct dependence on China for magnets: 36.5% (calc_notes.md §4c / one_hop_dependence.csv, 2024, tonnes)
    Number 2
    one-hop dependence for magnets: 67.8% (calc_notes.md §4c / one_hop_dependence.csv, 2024, tonnes)

    Japan halved its direct reliance on Chinese magnets after the 2010 shipment halt, but 73% of its hidden dependence now runs through Vietnam.

    Source: Pietro, calc_notes.md §4c; Anna's texts, "One hop away"; Yury's news file, items 6 and 9.

    hub

    Vietnam

    Number 1
    own dependence on China for magnets: 86.7% (calc_notes.md §4c, D_j, 2024, tonnes)
    Number 2
    top-5 hub: yes, for Japan (#1, 73.1% of Japan's one-hop gap) and the EU27 (#3, 13.3%) (calc_notes.md §4c, 2024, tonnes)

    Vietnam supplies about a quarter of Japan's magnets but buys 87% of its own from China: Japan's diversification after 2010 largely runs back to China.

    Source: Pietro, calc_notes.md §4c; Anna's texts, "One hop away".

    supplier

    Myanmar

    Number 1
    share of China's compound imports: 57.8% by weight, 59.5% by value (china_imports_by_stage_partner.csv, 2024, tonnes)
    Number 2
    could cover 162% of China's compound sales to the West (44,348 t), but all of it now goes to China (coverage_split_2012_2024.csv, 2024, tonnes; draft, to verify)

    Myanmar supplies the feedstock China's refiners need: more than half of China's imported compounds, so the mining happens abroad while the refining stays in China.

    Source: Pietro, china_imports_by_stage_partner.csv and coverage_split_2012_2024.csv; Framework, Part B, B6 (Reuters, 2021); Anna's texts, "Sovereignty on paper".
