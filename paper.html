Sovereignty on paper

For compounds, the predictor says other countries already trade enough to replace all of China's sales to the West. But look at where that supply goes today: Myanmar, Laos and Malaysia sell much of it to China, to Chinese refiners. And for magnets, the stage that matters most, other countries could replace only about 13% of China's sales to the West, even on paper.

This is also how China eases the environmental burden at home: the mining happens abroad, the refining stays in China. To cover the gap, the West would have to outbid China for that feedstock, then separate and refine it itself, using the know-how China banned from export in 2023. The capacity to replace China exists on paper; in practice, much of it depends on China.

Findings

What you see
for compounds, other countries already sell more than twice what China sells to the West (237% in 2024, by weight), mainly Myanmar and Laos. But 93% of that supply goes to China itself; without it, they could cover only about 16% of the gap, less than in 2012 (19%). For magnets, coverage is only 13%.
What it means
most of the "replacement" supply exists only on paper. To use it, the West would have to take it away from Chinese buyers and then refine it itself, which it has little capacity to do today.
Careful
trade data show where goods go, not who has the plants to refine them. The predictor is a "what if" exercise, not a forecast of what will happen.
