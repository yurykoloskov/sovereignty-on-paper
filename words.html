How Beijing justified the shift

We read 576 Chinese government documents on rare earths, from 1999 to 2026, and measured how they frame the issue. Until 2011 the language was about managing trade: export quotas, licences and state trading made up 62% of the framing vocabulary in 1999–2005 and 40% in 2006–11.

Then the justification changed. Mining had left landslides, polluted rivers and sick workers, and Beijing presented cutting raw-material output as a need of China's environment and its people's health. Environmental language peaked at 32% of framing in 2012–15, while China defended its export quotas at the WTO and published its first white paper on the industry (June 2012). China lost the case in 2014 and dropped the quotas in 2015, but it kept the argument: control moved to mining quotas and state consolidation, and trade language fell to 9%.

The words matched a real change: since 2018 China has bought more rare-earth compounds abroad than it sells (in 2024, about 77,000 tonnes imported against 42,000 exported). What it did not give up was control, of refining, of magnets and of the technology behind them.

From 2023 the documents start to say what that position is for. Rare earths are no longer described as a polluting industry to discipline, but as "strategic minerals" tied to national security: controlled through export licences, backed by state reserves, and bargained over in trade talks with the United States. The phrase "export control" never appears in these documents before 2016; in 2023–26 it appears 56 times. Security and strategic-resource language, almost absent before 2023, now makes up about 31% of the framing.

The environmental argument justified building the second monopoly; the security argument shows it being used.

Findings

What you see
trade-management language falls from 62% to 5% of framing; environmental language peaks at 32% in 2012–15; security language appears only after 2022.
What it means
Beijing explained the second monopoly first as an environmental choice, then as a security one; the monopoly itself never stopped.
Careful
these are counts of words in official documents, not proof of intentions. After 2023 the corpus holds more news and spokesperson statements, but a robustness check shows the shift is not only a change of genre.

Also in the data: trade-management vocabulary falls from about 50% in 2009 to about 21% in 2011, years before quotas were abolished in 2015. High-tech and value-chain framing holds at 15–28% in every period; security sits on top of the development story, and what gets replaced is trade management. The Premier's work and budget reports mention rare earths or strategic minerals only 3 times in 28 years; policy is set and announced by ministries.

Source: NLP report "How China talks about rare earths, 1999–2026", §1, §3.3, §3.5 and fig4 (the 2009 and 2011 values were read off the figure). US comparison (87 documents, preliminary): "strategic resource and reserves" reaches 51% of US framing vocabulary in 2016–22, while China reaches 21% only in 2023–26.
